## 3. Problem

**Superheadline:** The nonprofit website problem

**Headline:** Your website is talking to everyone at once.

A nonprofit website has more jobs than a business website. The people who use your programs need to find them and access them. Donors need to see the impact. Grant reviewers evaluate you in a language of their own. Volunteers, staff, and partners each need something too. So the site tries to say everything, every stakeholder adds a piece, and the result comes out bloated and unclear. The message that matters most gets buried under all the others. You should be able to trust that your website does the job you built it for: making the mission clear to every person who needs to understand it.

---

## 4. Solution

**Headline:** Start with a Blueprint. Then build.

SiteFlyer starts every nonprofit engagement with a process designed to get to know your organization and what matters to you. We call it the Website Blueprint. Before anyone designs a page, we work out who your audiences are, what each of them needs from the site, what pages it takes, and how to frame every conversation. Your stakeholders get a voice at the table, and the Blueprint turns those voices into one unified plan.

Then we build to the plan:

- **Sharp design** that looks professional and earns trust, so people take your organization seriously the moment they land.
- **Smart copy** that says what you do and why it matters, clearly enough to turn a visitor into a participant, a donor, or a volunteer.

## Appendix B — Structural Decisions

1. **The page sells the Blueprint first.** Solution is built around the Website Blueprint per the 9/14 context; the standard project appears as the secondary path in Who It's For. This inverts the main site's product order deliberately.
2. **The search pillar is omitted.** Messaging's third pillar (Growing reach) and the Plan's Grow step describe the subscription product. The nonprofit offer is project-based, so the page runs on the design and copy pillars only, and the Plan is adapted from Plan/Build/Grow to Blueprint/Build/Launch-and-support.
3. **Problem leads with the External layer, nonprofit-adapted.** The multi-audience/committee problem carries the emotional weight for this buyer; the paragraph lands on the Messaging Philosophical frame ("trust that the website does the job you built it for"), adapted from lead delivery to mission clarity.
4. **Who It's For became a two-card packages section ("Two Ways to Start").** Per Matt 9/14: two side-by-side cards modeled on the new site's pricing-page card treatment, Blueprint First and Website Project. No subscription option appears on this page. Both cards state that strategy is included; they differ in depth. Card anatomy (eyebrow / name / who-it's-for / how-it-works / includes / price / CTA) is a standard structure drafted without access to the new site's repo; verify against the actual pricing cards and adjust fields to match.
5. **Subscription framing is absent by design.** Payment flexibility language from Messaging (pay over time) is excluded; the page speaks in annual-budget, grant, and donor-funding terms per the 9/14 context.
6. **Source note.** Locked Positioning and Messaging are built around the local-service subscription buyer. Nonprofit-specific claims on this page trace to Matt's 9/14 context and Profile B in Positioning. Recommend a nonprofit addendum to the Messaging doc after this page locks, consistent with homepage-leads-docs-follow.
7. **Excluded from Jesse's draft.** The intermediary/cohort product (sponsored business websites, visibility cohorts, technical-assistance programs) is out of scope for this page pending the business decision.
